## Terrain and level settings

- A cell remains **1 unit**. Each placement box is **24 × 24 × 24 units (XYZ)**, with Y up. Model width and length may each occupy up to 24 units; model height may exceed 24 units.
- Each level has a ground layer **one box (24 units) high by default**. Support carving areas out for lakes and streams, and higher scenery mounds. **Latest decision: player defences must sit at the enemy path height**, superseding the earlier raised-defence requirement. Terrain regions align to 24 × 24 boxes horizontally; ground and water heights use 1-unit vertical steps. Enemy paths stay flat and dry. Sparse per-box ground/water source and editing are implemented; translucent water with visible beds, refraction and ripple glints is now approved and implemented in the engine. Gameplay consequences of other terrain elevations remain undecided.
- **Time of day is authored per level**, with no day/night cycle. The latest approval adds an **N day/night toggle in the visual preview**: only sky-light/ambient colours change, keeping light direction, shadow matrices and intensity settings fixed. The flat night backdrop uses the ambient colour. The preview override does not change saved time-of-day data or define final gameplay controls.
- **Weather is authored per level and stays fixed for that level**. The player cannot change it, and there are no dynamic weather transitions. Effects may animate while the selected weather remains unchanged. Zone A uses rain, enabled or disabled by an author switch on each level. Zone C uses a snowstorm through the same author switch. Zone D uses gently falling purple leaves with muted rusty-orange variety and matching path-edge litter. Zone B stays clear for now. The approved Weather On/Off button temporarily overrides weather in the visual preview; it does not change saved level data or establish a player gameplay control. C snow falls downward with bounded flutter and includes matte settled path-edge patches when enabled. Rain makes path surfaces reflective; weather particles respond to existing sky, ambient and local light sources.

## Approved rendering capabilities

| Capability | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Depth prepass | Begin scene rendering with a depth-only pass, before colour shading, to minimise expensive overdraw. Profile the combined pass cost rather than assuming a guaranteed speedup. |
| Dynamic lighting and shadows | Support dynamic lighting, with at least one sky light in every level that casts shadows. Fixed time of day does not prevent moving objects or lights from updating their lighting and shadows. |
| Emissive light sources | Some emissive model faces must illuminate their surroundings. Make this an explicit opt-in flag, independent of the face's emission value. Emission alone must not automatically make every emissive face a light source. |
| Screen-space reflections | Support SSR using the approved per-face reflectiveness value. |
| Particles | Support particle effects. Muzzle flashes and local shot sparks are now approved and implemented; Zone A rain, Zone C snow and Zone D purple/rust autumn leaves are implemented; further effect designs remain to be specified. |
| Instancing | Use instanced rendering wherever suitable to reduce draw calls and share geometry across repeated models and parts. |

Face colour, **emission 0–1**, and **reflectiveness 0–1** remain required throughout authoring, meshing, export, and rendering. Preserve the explicit `castsLight` light-source flag through that same pipeline, including face overrides and mesh merging. The representation of emitted light and its performance budget remain engineering decisions; the flag does not prescribe a separate runtime light object for every quad.

**Game models must remain texture-free. Visual-effect textures may be created when needed**, as explicitly approved by the user. GUI textures and GPU render/depth/data textures are also allowed. This permission changes the available techniques for approved effects; it does not approve unrelated effects. Current muzzle flashes and sparks use procedural shapes without texture assets.

Latest appearance decision: **water and rain-wet paths are reflective at present**. Enabled Zone A rain gives path stone reflectiveness 0.4 and mortar 0.1, with slightly blue edge puddles at 0.85 using clean surface replacements and existing SSR. All active model materials, including face override materials, are set to reflectiveness 0; source generators and new-model defaults agree. This also removes the model shader's reflectiveness-driven specular highlight. The format and renderer retain reflection support for future approved use. The stream's reflectiveness remains 0.65.

## Scope and performance

Only the requirements above and other explicitly recorded approvals define the scope. **Fog, haze, and any additional effects require approval.** Weather support does not implicitly approve fog or haze. Artwork does not grant approval to implement every effect it depicts.

Prioritise engine performance, reuse objects and buffers, and pool transient objects where appropriate. Measure representative scenes and allocation behaviour. Hardware targets, frame-time budgets, light counts, particle counts, shadow quality, and SSR quality have not been set.
