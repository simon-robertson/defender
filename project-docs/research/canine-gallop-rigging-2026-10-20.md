**Status: research and recommendations only.** The user finds the current Dog sprint unconvincing, with too little change in body altitude, supplies an extended-flight greyhound photograph, and suggests fast straights with rapid slowing at corners. This note audits the production rig and proposes a review process. It changes no animation, models, movement rates, routes or gameplay. The earlier roughly **2× normal escape speed** remains the approved working cap; a faster straight-line cap is a separate proposal. Existing running/clearance tests establish the sampled physical behaviour, not acceptance of its canine appearance.

## Conclusion

The present Dog can perform a much clearer gallop without a new renderer, but its single rigid torso/head cannot reproduce the reference's change between a long extended body and a compact, flexed body. First make an isolated comparison with corrected contact ordering, distinct collected and extended flight poses, visible flight height, and speed-dependent stride length using the existing asset. If the user wants the reference's torso articulation, propose an authored mechanical chest/pelvis joint and independent head before changing the accepted model. **Weighted skinning and a GPU animation pipeline are not required by either option.** The existing hierarchical rigid-part system already provides the relevant transform operations.

## Recommended animation design — proposals

### Contact sequence and two different suspensions

Use one explicitly chosen rotary lead and mirror it when required; for example FR → FL → RL → RR around the body. Assign event times rather than changing four offsets until the motion happens to look plausible. Use the source's lead/non-lead terminology in diagnostics so the leg labels cannot hide an accidental transverse sequence. Exact event fractions and any lead change during turning remain artistic choices for the prototype.

Create separate poses around the contact events:

1. Fore support/landing: leading/trailing contacts remain distinguishable; elbows and body load rather than all legs snapping straight together.
2. Collected flight: forelegs fold and recover under the chest while hindlegs swing forward beneath the torso; the compact silhouette must differ visibly from the next flight.
3. Hind support and push: rear chains extend as the body moves over the planted feet, without foot sliding or knee inversion.
4. Extended flight: front paws reach ahead, rear paws trail, and the torso is visibly airborne. This is the phase supplied by the user's photo, not a pose held throughout the run.

These are animation design goals, not a claim that every contact is isolated in time. Brief overlap within a limb pair can remain if the chosen source-derived timing permits it. Use slower playback and contact diagrams to check the sequence; use normal playback from the game overview to judge whether it actually reads.

### Body trajectory and speed coupling

Replace the almost constant-height appearance with a continuous, contact-aware trajectory: visible compression under load, push-off, a smooth flight apex, then landing. A ballistic-like arc is a useful artistic starting shape during each unsupported interval; its amplitude and timing must fit actual paw trajectories and the mechanical proportions. Do not give both suspensions identical bounces by default or infer the body's mass-centre trajectory from a lifted toe. Choose body-height excursion visually and from reach clearance, not by copying metres from greyhound experiments.

During stance, inverse kinematics must solve the ground contact from the posed hip; choose body height/pitch that keeps all supporting chains within a safe extension margin. During suspension, raise foot targets with the body as necessary and permit purposeful tuck/reach rather than forcing every paw to remain near the route plane. The low stance root can stay compatible with the present short limbs while the unsupported root rises farther. Whole-body clearance, overhead scenery, corridor width, existing impact displacement and the larger animated envelope still have to pass.

Drive the gait from accepted movement, not an independent time loop that runs while a queue is stopped. Use a continuous phase accumulator with a speed-dependent stride length/cadence rule, maintaining `speed = cadence × stride length` on a straight. Let the run primarily lengthen and open the stride before raising cadence excessively. Do not recompute phase as `distance / changingStrideLength`: that would move existing contacts and can jump phase. Freeze or settle gait appropriately at a stop and blend into/out of run at contact-safe moments. Animal cadence numbers are reference context, not proposed mandatory game settings.

### Rig options

| Option | What it can demonstrate | Limitation / approval boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing thirteen parts | Correct contact events, larger flight clearance, more distinct paw tuck/reach, varied front/rear swing paths, contact-aware root height/pitch and turn behaviour. No asset edit. | The back remains a rigid slab and the head follows it. It can be a credible mechanical gallop approximation, but cannot promise the flexed/extended torso of the photograph. |
| Authored torso articulation | Split existing solid geometry into a front chest, rear pelvis and separately controlled head, with a deliberate exposed mechanical hinge/bridge at the waist; front chains attach to chest and rear chains to pelvis. Chest and pelvis can pitch relative to each other; head can counter-pitch modestly. An extra short middle segment is optional if a two-piece back makes an abrupt hinge. | **Recommended if close reference matching is required; model approval remains open.** Preserve neutral silhouette, palette, sensors and texture-free style. Artist must design the seam and exposed joint; arbitrary mesh slicing can expose holes or intersecting shell faces. An exact part count is premature. |
| Additional leg joints | Separate hock/metatarsal control or front/rear-specific chain geometry could improve recovery silhouettes if the existing two-link limbs remain visibly limiting. | Optional later revision, not a prerequisite for the first comparison. Adds solver, authoring and clearance complexity. Do not invent an organic tail, ears or new proportions. |

For the articulated option, use a stable navigation/root transform for route progress, then a bounded body presentation transform with chest/pelvis/head channels. Root rise is not the same operation as spinal flexion. Derive each hip from its full composed parent matrix and solve each contact in that parent's space. Compose missile-hit response and running rather than letting the generic `body` write overwrite the new channels. Preserve a compatible `body` aim anchor or deliberately migrate its contract; update actual aim velocity, lights, smoke/explosion origins, targeting, collision bounds and cleanup together. None of this requires weighted deformation.

Head counter-motion is an artistic control recommendation to retain sensor readability while the chest pitches. The sources above do not specify an exact head-stabilisation law. Likewise, chest/pelvis angle magnitudes and whether the mechanical spine also yaws into a turn remain open visual choices.

### Fast straights and deliberate corners

The user's suggestion is appropriate to test on these compact right-angle routes. Keep the current 2× straight cap for the comparison. Anticipate a bend, reduce speed over a short visible approach, pass the bend on a supported path with shorter steps and reduced flight, then accelerate as the route opens. A rapid *continuous* slowdown gives weight; a discontinuous velocity change gives a snap. The exact slowdown, braking distance and recovery rate remain provisional, with both an isolated side view and the real overview needed for review.

A practical engineering starting rule is a speed cap from curvature, `vCorner = sqrt(aLateralBudget / max(abs(curvature), epsilon))`, combined with a look-ahead braking constraint `vNow² <= vCorner² + 2*aBrake*distanceToBend`. These are proposed controller relationships, not fitted canine constants. Use actual curve arc distance and consistent units; canonical path progress is not automatically world distance on the rounded curve. A short arc-length/curvature lookup built at path setup is one bounded option. Keep actual tangent/yaw derivatives, published velocity, gait phase and aim prediction consistent, including while the path blend changes.

Lean should respond smoothly to actual lateral acceleration and speed, stay within geometry/reach limits, then recover without a sudden reversal at the corner. A clamped visual bank inspired by `atan(v²*curvature / gArt)` is optional; `gArt` is artistic tuning, not an assigned world gravity. Shorter stance-to-stance advances and a less aerial gait at the corner should carry the slowdown rather than a large roll alone. Retain traffic braking, merge ownership and conservative escape classification. Do not increase the source route's usable corridor or move its endpoints.

There is a gameplay boundary here: changing corner progress or animation height can alter real weapon opportunities, missile contact and time to exit. Keeping a 2× straight cap does not by itself preserve current wave timing. Production integration must recheck the escape proof's damage/time assumptions and the mixed campaign witness. This research note authorises neither those changes nor a greater-than-2× cap.

## Proposed staged review and acceptance checks

1. **Isolated visual prototype:** retain production assets and simulation defaults. Compare the present run against the improved existing-rig interpretation at the same translation speed. Show straight, start/stop and single-corner loops, a slow-motion contact view, and the normal play camera. Display phase, contact flags and actual body/sole height for diagnosis. The existing `/tools/escape-run-review.html` is a possible disposable host; do not reset the user's player run.
2. **Choose the visual scope:** judge whether the existing mechanical torso is sufficient. If it still prevents the requested collected/extended silhouette, show a non-production articulated torso proposal with neutral and extreme poses; obtain approval of the model revision before replacing the accepted asset. Avoid promising exact greyhound motion from unchanged short-legged proportions.
3. **Production integration only when authorised:** retain shared `/game/` walk defaults, profiles/economy, layouts and frozen backup. Retest the code dependencies actually changed. No new renderer, enemy type or campaign content follows automatically from accepting the gait.

Review should establish all of the following:

- At normal playback the Dog visibly compresses, collects, pushes and extends; it does not read as a fast walk, a rigid hopping box or legs cycling beneath an almost level chassis. The user's appearance review is decisive.
- A full cycle shows the chosen four-foot event order and both distinct unsupported intervals, with actual transformed soles above the floor during suspension. The photograph's extended pose occurs as part of a cycle.
- Planted paws remain fixed and flat within numeric tolerance; chain reach stays inside the valid domain with margin; no knee flips, joint snapping, ground penetration, shell/limb overlap or exposed seam problems appear in dense sampled poses.
- Start, acceleration, braking, corners, queue stop/restart, impact recovery, death, pause, reset and unload remain continuous and finite. Check 30/60/120 Hz equivalence where motion is integrated; a few screenshots cannot establish this.
- The complete animated envelope fits supported route/bridge geometry and scenery clearance, including merged traffic. Recheck actual target geometry, eye lighting, smoke/death origins and published aim velocity if torso channels change.
- Record changed part/draw counts, upload bytes and allocation behaviour in a representative wave. Use measured costs; no new hardware budget or performance claim is assigned here.
- If translation or target geometry changes, repeat relevant escape-proof/traffic/missile checks and the existing all-wave unarmed/mixed-strategy witnesses. Report any timing difference rather than claiming visual work cannot affect balance.
