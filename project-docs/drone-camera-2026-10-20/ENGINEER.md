## Motion, framing and safety

The initial view starts at a clear central overhead position. Every subsequent flight uses 120 Hz camera steps from the engine clock, with 34-unit/s cruise, 24-unit/s² acceleration/braking, 1.25-rad/s pan, 0.8-rad/s tilt, ±1.35-radian pitch and 42–76° vertical FOV. Zoom changes at most 9°/s. Long render gaps cap camera integration at 0.25 seconds; zero engine elapsed time and pause freeze the pose. Settling keeps the camera position and final action composition while smoothly finishing aim/lens motion.

A fixed 72-candidate search runs on a 0.8-second plan interval and chooses low (ground+20), middle (+54) and high (+96) stations. After at least eight seconds of useful holding, changing the height can provide another action angle; it keeps the azimuth when that column is clear. The altitude preference changes on fourteen-second bands. Travel toward a new subject, obstruction or distance can also cause a move. Subjects stay selected at least seven seconds while valid, then progress, participating fire, proximity and a small flyer preference inform the next shot. Composition reads real aimX/aimY/aimZ, so airborne enemies are framed at their real height. Five subject-envelope rays and nine aspect-aware foreground rays reject obstructed compositions. These periodic quality scores describe planned framing; they do not prove an unobstructed complete image during travel.

Expanded-box swept segment tests use the exact existing scenery/ground/deck BVH, a separate authored-water height slab, world boundaries and a 2.5-unit camera hull. A clear destination alone is insufficient. Every low destination requires an open sky column. Clear direct transitions must also have a static open corridor overhead; otherwise the reusable route climbs vertically, crosses above the canopy and descends through the destination column. There is no mid-flight teleport fallback. If a safe route is unavailable, it waits/replans.

The controller reuses `DroneObstacles` for current posed actor bounds, full defence turning envelopes and bounded motion prediction, populated with the current finite wave actors and wildlife only (hidden pool aliases are excluded). Complete authored airborne routes additionally receive conservative all-angle envelopes plus a four-unit margin; these reservations include future Moths before they spawn and keep descent/escape columns out of their eventual flight lanes. Cruise height remains above both static canopy and those reserved lanes. This fixed scene-owned lane storage fixed an observed A2 test failure where a descending camera stopped in an approaching Moth's height band. For other forecast encroachments, a currently clear upward column permits an early climb with current swept actor gates, rather than treating the entire future envelope around the start as an impassable wall. These are conservative camera controls, not general collision physics or universal avoidance guarantees for arbitrary authored actors.

## Storage and validation

Reusable camera state uses 288 typed bytes, plus 4 bytes per terrain cell for water heights, the existing actor-envelope cache, and finite airborne-route segment boxes. Each route owns exactly nine Float64 slots (three waypoints). Shot height tables are shared; ordinary candidate/route plans reuse existing storage. Scene bind/refresh releases actor/world references and lane storage. No GPU pass, image, buffer or dependency is added by this controller.
