## Firing and target ownership

`game/missile-preview.js` exposes `MissilePreview(world, onPoseChange, onVisible, targets, onHit, {onImpact, onLaunch, onTrail})`, its `states`, `incomingDamage`, `eligible`, `advance`, `resetTargets` and `dispose`. The final options object and its callbacks are optional. The actor factory provides 36 fixed `guided-missile` actors, loaded only in the missile studies. The shared planner selects targets alongside other defences, with one ranking operation per frame. Guided missiles provide no speculative future-damage credit.

Admission checks the living target, finite height and inclusive horizontal centre distance. The next seated socket must have a scenery-clear predicted flight: shared 120 Hz boost/coast/predictive-turn integration sweeps the whole-model clearance sphere. Actual forecast body contact ends the check successfully before a later ground obstacle; earlier terrain, scenery and other defences reject it. A passed point, off-map trajectory or horizon without predicted contact may still be admitted if unobstructed; this is not guaranteed-hit certification. A reused scratch record and same-frame/socket/aim/velocity cache bound this query. No straight ground-level ray is required, so low obstacles can still be cleared by the loft. Forecast velocity is an estimate; physical contacts still decide the result after a later turn or death. A successful launch consumes its seated actor; that same actor becomes the flying missile. Reload starts only on the last actual launch, prevents firing, and completes with the entire magazine. Target changes do not refill it. If the pool cannot supply a complete reload, the empty launcher waits; partial refills and stealing active flights are prohibited.

The subsequently approved silo accounting uses those same fixed flight slots as a ledger. `MissileFlightPool.incomingDamage(target)` and the wrapper's `incomingDamage(target)` sum each launched slot's captured strength, including missiles launched by the querying silo. A claim requires the exact living target object to remain in the current roster with the original lap and finite root/torso coordinates, a BOOST/COAST/HOMING phase younger than eight seconds, and neither `orphaned` nor `passedAim`. Loaded rounds, future magazine shots and unsuccessful launches contribute zero. The query validates current target state immediately because shared planning runs before flight simulation; it does not retain a separate map, reservation or damage forecast.

Silo acquisition and the live pre-trigger gate require **current health > incoming damage**, with no 125% margin on actual missiles. At 225 HP, three 75-damage launches cover the target exactly; subsequent silos in the same frame hold their rounds. At 226 HP, another round is permitted. A silo's own earlier flights count. A covered target spends no ammunition and does not advance the launch deadline: a partial magazine waits until real firing resumes, and reload still starts only when empty. Unknown/invalid health remains unbounded, matching shared planning, rather than inventing a lethal threshold.

A known missed destination stops contributing as soon as `passedAim` is set, even while the missile continues toward ground. Death, lap/roster invalidation, expiry, stalls, reset and disposal likewise invalidate or release claims. Enemy/static contacts retire the slot before visibility, impact and damage callbacks, preventing the arriving missile from being counted alongside its applied damage. Surviving flights remain independently credited. This accounting is an estimate of assigned damage, not a guarantee that a physical missile will hit; a previously missed missile can still collide with its original enemy without regaining a claim.

The shared planner subtracts actual incoming damage once from current HP, then applies its existing 125% margin only to remaining health covered by speculative gun forecasts. Missile magazine capacity and future launches still provide no forecast. [Shared targeting policy](defence-targeting.md) records reservation and mixed-family behavior. Each query scans at most 36 existing slots, reusing all storage; the new gates add no per-frame allocation, GPU buffer or new flight field.

A living-target flight keeps the exact target object and route lap from launch for contact/death checks, and continues beyond acquisition range. Before passing its destination, removal, route wrapping or invalid coordinates still discard that flight. At reignition it begins powered interception, refreshing the predicted destination only while the assigned target remains living, valid and before terminal cutoff or passed-aim. Four bounded acceleration-aware refinements use authored future torso motion for undisturbed flyers and current aim velocity for other motion. When the target dies, the missile instead freezes its final finite XYZ aim point (or the last valid actual sample if the death pose is unavailable) and drops its target-object reference. This death rule takes precedence over an earlier predicted intercept: before commitment it prevents prediction, and during the approach it changes the destination once to the final actual point. Before terminal cutoff the missile can steer toward that final point. After cutoff the destination still changes for accounting, but the physical trajectory cannot redirect or reignite: the latched inertial-flight rule takes precedence. Death itself never snaps position, attitude or velocity; later movement, reset or removal of the enemy object cannot alter the retained point.

A swept one-unit nose-arrival tolerance or crossing of the destination plane marks an empty point as passed. The missile retains its attack direction and continues forward without turning back. Once terminal cutoff has occurred it stays unpowered and preserves its incoming velocity through subsequent contact or release. The same rule applies to an empty last-known position. A living assigned target can still receive damage through real surface contact, but later death/removal/wrapping only releases the target reference after the destination has been passed. Scenery contact, off-map release or the eight-second lifetime terminates the remaining flight. Passing a point itself produces no explosion or damage.

The missile-only damage callback is `onHit(target, strength, vx, vy, vz)`, carrying actual incoming velocity as scalar arguments; existing turret/Arc callbacks still supply two arguments. A physical enemy contact first invokes `onImpact(x, y, z, nx, ny, nz)` at the exact swept surface point with its outward normal, then applies strength once. Static contact invokes only `onImpact`; it never deals damage. `onLaunch(x, y, z)` fires only on a successful launch, at the transformed socket X/Z and deck-mouth Y, seven units above the seated root. Loading, refilling and resetting produce no launch smoke. Both callbacks feed the existing pooled `DamageEffects` implementation; death effects retain priority if the hit is lethal. `onTrail(x, y, z)` supplies the actual interpolated tail at 24 puffs/second during powered HOMING. Terminal engine cutoff stops emission; existing grey puffs grow and fade over 2 seconds. They share the shared 384-smoke pool (expanded from128 in the longer-trail follow-up), with no new shader, pass or bloom source; the larger existing particle buffer adds12,288 GPU bytes; overflow drops puffs without evicting existing smoke. Reset clears trail cadence and particle flags. There is no retargeting, splash damage, launch damage or audio.

## Three continuous phases

The immutable tuning lives in `shared/missile-flight.js`; `engine/missile-flight.js` owns simulation and contact.

1. **Boost:** vertical velocity 64 units/second for 0.50 seconds, with exhaust. The 32-unit departure exceeds the authored ten-unit straight clearance.
2. **Coast-turn:** exhaust stops for 0.40 seconds. Momentum continues under gravity 40 units/second² while attitude rotates toward a prospective intercept at up to 360°/second. The centre rises another 22.4 units and ends with 48 units/second upward velocity. Rotation does not freeze or relocate the centre of mass.
3. **Predictive attack (runtime `HOMING` phase):** exhaust restarts; while powered, refresh the finite intercept and compensate lateral momentum through the existing eased attitude controller. The command is 180 units/s, acceleration 630 units/s² and turn cap 240°/s with the existing per-round variation. No approach slowdown is added. Within 22 units, cutoff additionally requires forward velocity, at least 98% cruise speed and a velocity line within one unit of the lead point. Once latched, freeze physical steering/acceleration and retain inertial flight. Close contact can precede safe cutoff.

The prediction starts from a finite constant-velocity intercept and makes four bounded acceleration-aware time refinements. For undisturbed airborne actors, five authored curve/yaw/bank samples predict the torso without mutating its rig; active impact motion falls back to actual analytic torso velocity. Ground and legacy actors also use finite torso/root velocity. Range remains horizontal actor-root distance. Actual swept mesh contact decides damage; later motion changes, death or obstacles can still defeat the forecast.

Rigid orientation follows local +Y and transports the right axis to avoid roll snaps near vertical. Source sockets and the tail exhaust origin come from `shared/missile-model.js`; quarter-turn launcher roots are respected. Flights expire after eight seconds.

Frames up to one second are integrated in steps no larger than 1/120 second, split at phase boundaries. A bounded loop also tolerates tiny fractional remainders; a prior epsilon bug that could prevent progress has been corrected and covered by exact-boundary and uneven-frame checks. Frames longer than one second discard existing flights without damage, advance the shared clocks by the complete delta and permit at most one current launch per launcher. Missed launches are not replayed. No independent timer or animation loop is introduced.

## Collision and practical limits

Enemy contact sweeps the missile's nose between substeps against actual exposed quads of its assigned actor. A world actor AABB, then part-local AABBs, reject distant segments before quad tests. Part bounds are cached at scene construction; world bounds refresh only while flights exist. Physical translation samples the actor root matrix at its authored centre `[12,0,12]`, independently of torso aim samples. This includes real body bob while excluding tilt-induced aim movement and yaw's pivot-compensating matrix translation. That root motion is interpolated across the frame. Bone orientation uses the current articulated pose, so this is not continuous swept skeletal rotation.

Static obstruction uses the existing terrain/scenery BVH and a conservative enclosing sphere of radius √30 (about 5.48 units) around the missile centre. Sweeping against expanded boxes prevents thin-obstacle tunnelling, but can reject near-corner routes before the visible missile touches them. The earliest contact fraction wins across all BVH leaves and the assigned enemy, independent of traversal order. The static event is projected onto the original occupied box face and clamped within that face; a ground explosion therefore sits at the actual ground height, not at the elevated enclosing-sphere centre. Enemy normals come from the contacted quad transformed by its current part matrix. Unknown already-overlapping faces and exterior map boundaries do not invent impact events. A missile whose centre leaves the map is released without an explosion, as are expiry, stalls, reset, disposal and invalidation before arrival.

The launcher departure itself is independently verified against exact authored cells by the model tests. Flight paths are locally guided, not obstacle-planned; scenery can stop them with a cosmetic impact and no damage. The static BVH excludes actors/defences, but live posed defence cuboids are now swept separately by TODO 41 in both launch admission and actual flight. Ignore only the firing launcher; other defence solids can stop a missile with a cosmetic impact. Real part openings remain open, with the same conservative body-radius clearance. Other-enemy interception is not added.

## Pools, rendering and release

There are 36 reusable actor/control slots shared by loaded and flying rounds. The current three-launcher scene loads twelve. The approved cadence/reload/lifetime permits at most twenty simultaneous flights by Tactician's conservative count; the 24-flight allowance leaves four slots beyond that current-preview requirement. This is a bounded study budget, not a campaign-wide capacity promise.

All missiles share one 34-quad mesh and one instanced draw group: measured vertex/index storage is **5,984 + 816 = 6,800 bytes**, with **2,304 GPU bytes** for 36 instance matrices. Existing renderer visibility compaction removes free slots from depth, shadow and colour draws. Loaded-to-flight transitions allocate no model, mesh or buffer. Each slot has reusable aim, last-actual-position and destination-direction scalars, plus committed/orphaned/passed/cutoff flags. Terminal cutoff adds one reusable boolean per slot and no arrays or GPU storage. Contact position/normal scratch scalars are shared by the pool. Contact records, owner references, matrices and fixed flame storage are reused; there are no per-shot object constructions or per-substep arrays. The new callbacks do not add a flight pool, renderer buffer or shader; their bounded visual storage belongs to `DamageEffects`.

Each powered missile contributes two procedural records. Boost retains the tapered, flickering warm flame (nominal width 1.8, length 12, alpha 1) and compact core (width 1.2). Attack uses 1.3× width/core and 1.5× length: width 2.34, length 18 and core 1.56. `MISSILE_ATTACK_THRUST` centralises the attack multipliers. The existing depth-tested transparent shot shader and selective bloom source shader render them. Coast and latched terminal flight contribute zero records. Smoke is a separate short burst at launch only; there is no opaque flame geometry or long trail.

The approved exhaust lighting adds exactly one warm point source per powered slot, three units behind the tail along the actual missile attitude. Tuning is radius 32, boost strength 3 and attack strength 4.5, multiplied by the existing 0.8–1 flame pulse, with linear RGB `[1,.61,.26]`. `MISSILE_THRUST_LIGHT` centralises the base values and `MISSILE_ATTACK_THRUST` supplies the stronger attack intensity. BOOST and powered HOMING contribute light; COAST, latched terminal flight, loaded rounds, free slots and completed flights do not. The same existing point-light response illuminates opaque surfaces, water, smoke, debris, rain, snow and leaves. It is an unshadowed local source like the existing muzzle/model lights; it can spill through source geometry or nearby walls and adds no shadow pass.

The shared point-light capacity is **69**: all eight muzzle, four damage/explosion and 21 model slots remain available, followed by up to 36 exhaust slots derived from the fixed missile actor capacity. Arc tube sources retain their separate buffer. `pool.prepare()` packs exhaust sources into a reused **1,168-byte CPU array**, owned only by missile scenes. The existing shared GPU uniform and CPU merge array each grow from **1,072 to 2,224 bytes**; this fixed 1,152-byte increase applies even without missiles. No separate exhaust-light GPU buffer, binding, shader formula or rendering pass is added. Actual source counts control shader loops and active-prefix uploads: **16 + 32 × count** bytes, including all point-light families. A final light going off writes the zero-count header once, then idle frames skip further light uploads. These resource counts do not establish a measured frame-time result.

The CPU flame array is **4,608 bytes** (72 × 16 floats). Its separate scene GPU buffer is also **4,608 bytes**, created only on the first burn. Each active missile uploads 128 bytes; only the active prefix is sent. One display draw and one bloom-source draw cover all active exhausts. Existing reduced bloom targets and pipelines are reused. Inactive C3 creates no thrust GPU buffer, upload or flame draw. These are resource/work counts, not measured CPU or GPU speed claims.

`setMissileThrust(null)` destroys the scene buffer and clears the CPU reference. `clearWorld` does the same; renderer teardown cannot retain the old pool. Controller disposal clears target, owner, actor, matrix and scenery references. **R** discards flights, clears reload/discard diagnostics and fully refills magazines using the existing actors, while preserving shared time and the current launch-gap clock. It preserves camera, lighting and weather through the existing application lifecycle.

Inspector fields include each launcher's ammo/capacity, reload deadline/count, pool deferrals, launch and impact diagnostics, plus pool collisions, expiry, target losses, stall discards and `offMap`. `pool.orphaned`, `pool.completedAtLastPosition` and `pool.missed` are cumulative since reset. The latter two now count passing an empty dead-target/live-target destination respectively; they no longer mean that the actor was retired there. A subsequent ground contact also increments `collisions`, so these counters are not mutually exclusive terminal outcomes. Active slots expose `committed`, `orphaned`, `passedAim`, `engineCutoff`, destination `aimX/aimY/aimZ` and last actual enemy sample `lastX/lastY/lastZ`. The counters, cutoff latch and per-slot destination state clear on reset/disposal, and disposal clears both callback references. Renderer inspection exposes `missileThrustBytes`, `missileThrustInstances`, `missileThrustUploadBytes`, `missileLights`, `missileLightCPUBytes`, `localLightUploadBytes` and `localLightUniformBytes`. The source object exposes `thrust.lightCount` and `thrust.lightData`; both light count and its packed header clear on reset, and renderer detachment clears the scene reference.
