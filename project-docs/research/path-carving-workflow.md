## Work through alternatives before carving

The following is the Tactician's future workflow, not a new campaign mechanic or a numerical user requirement. Spending more time means examining and revising alternatives, not assigning an arbitrary number of hours or running the same passing checks repeatedly.

1. **Read the untouched place.** Use Artist's frozen source and matching normal-overview capture. Map the lake/river/stream, connected dry ground, elevations, principal groves/ruins and supported wildlife anchors. Identify possible entry/exit relationships and regions a first route would bypass. Keep the landscape fully populated at this stage; do not ask Artist to hide a road or reserve lawns in the baseline.
2. **Sketch distinct journeys on the same landscape.** Compare routes with different landmark relationships, traversal order and engagement structure, not small coordinate shifts of the first candidate. Useful possibilities include a landform-following return, a longer journey between distinct courts, or a selective two-approach merge where it adds a real decision. Use enough alternatives to expose the main trade-offs; no fixed sketch count, route length, turn count or board-coverage quota is assigned. Mark speculative bridge crossings separately from dry-ground candidates.
3. **Draw the useful ground with each journey.** Show full 24×24 candidate areas and possible connected courts, including inside/outside-bend alternatives and spaces reached at different times. Vary their shapes and relationships. The intended result is a larger choice of useful positions, not merely a longer road beside the same three narrow clusters. Explain which additional sites create distinct decisions and which give equivalent, forgiving placement flexibility. Do not turn this authoring map into fixed build pads or a new eligibility rule.
4. **Compare coverage before selecting a route.** For each plausible area, place current weapon range circles and record journey intervals with ideal range and actual shot eligibility separately. Include the entry, central opportunities, all branches where proposed, and the route to its exit. Review how much useful ground survives at base range as well as upgraded range. If the attractive central pocket is tangent-only, or all alternatives cover the same interval, revise the sketch rather than changing weapon stats.
5. **Review the trade-offs with Artist and Lead.** Present alternatives at matching scale and camera framing with short rationales, not a single polished route that is costly to reconsider. Identify how each traverses the land, its useful placement options, remaining narrow corridors, strongest repeated-exposure area, independent late catch, likely dominant sites, scenery removals and unresolved engineering needs. Artist assesses preservation of the landscape and normal-overview readability; Tactician explains the tactical distinctions. Record why the selected option is preferable and what weaknesses remain.
6. **Carve the selected proposal within the later authorised scope.** Preserve the baseline and record each removal or smaller replacement by purpose: route travel, full defence footprint, attack sightline or overview visibility. Keep the water/terrain identity and coherent surrounding masses. If a viable journey requires a material landscape redesign or unsupported crossing, return that concrete issue to Lead instead of silently filling water, inventing support or shrinking choices until a weak route passes.
7. **Validate the actual carved geometry and alternate arrangements.** Check continuous supported travel, animated ground/air clearance, complete defence movement envelopes, actual per-species gun LOS/pitch, Arc contacts and missile flight where relevant. Test alternatives individually and check simultaneous clearance only for arrangements actually proposed; adjacent candidate boxes do not automatically mean adjacent rotating guns can coexist. Use unchanged mechanics and matched preview conditions to compare contributions. A finite combat demonstration does not prove campaign balance.
8. **Review the finished journey as a level.** Compare the untouched landscape and carve in the same normal overview, then follow the whole route. Confirm that the winding journey, multiple useful choices and preserved landscape read without needing orbiting. Remove or reduce scenery obscuring any intended full placement area, including alternatives. Technical correctness, internal design review and user acceptance are separate outcomes; record all three accurately.

## Describe placement choices precisely

For each comparison, record the site/area, weapon and stage, supported footprint, route/branch, eligible intervals in journey-distance units, and what it gains or gives up against another plausible site. Use a common representative probe for cross-site comparisons, then check the weapons and species the proposal actually demonstrates. Keep the following distinctions explicit:

| Question | Evidence that helps decide it |
| --- | --- |
| Is this useful ground? | A full dry same-height footprint, room for the model's movement, visible ground from the normal overview and positive actual attack opportunity. A remote empty box or a tangent point is insufficient. |
| Is a return a new opportunity? | Separate interval endpoints and the uncovered gap. Continuous coverage around a bend can be valuable, but is not leaving and re-entering range. Convert the gap to seconds using the relevant enemy speed; a very delayed revisit is a different choice from a quick return. |
| Do alternatives change a decision? | Earlier contact versus longer continuous fire; two separated encounters versus one uninterrupted encounter; one approach versus a common tail; different upgrade reach; or different practical weapon clearance. Equivalent intervals can still provide placement flexibility, but should not be presented as extra tactical patterns. |
| Does a late site add something? | A meaningful final journey interval that strong earlier sites miss or cover less well, with enough actual time and geometry to contribute. Use the full route and actual aim/cadence constraints, rather than a label such as `late`. |
| Does one court dominate everything? | Compare individual sites and plausible arrangements under matched assumptions. Record overlap between their covered intervals; do not sum overlapping route lengths as unique team coverage. A strong inner pocket may be desirable, but all other useful ground should not collapse into cosmetic alternatives. |
| Does the landscape still have room to play? | Map the accessible dry areas and actual useful courts together. Explain why bypassed regions remain scenery, or compare a journey that reaches them. Avoid both a narrow edge corridor by default and a road forced into every corner. |

The current base gun/Arc range is **48 units**, two placement boxes. Their upgrades use **72/96**; gun upgrade 2 remains inactive. Silos use **72/96/120**. Read the current [weapon contract](../design-doc.md#shared-attack-rules-and-current-weapon-values) at the time of the future task instead of freezing provisional balance in a new layout rule.

For unobstructed parallel straight sections, a base gun 24 units from each has real exposure to both; at 48 units from a straight it only touches the range boundary. The [saved range-spacing explanation](tower-defence-layouts.md#design-in-attack-range-not-in-board-percentages) shows why a visually broad inner court may depend on upgrades. Finite corners, muzzle height, mechanical pitch, moving targets and scenery require the actual checks. Arc surface contacts and curved missile flights cannot be certified by circles alone. A generator changes strength, not coverage; its base rule needs separate cardinal adjacent support ground.

## Water, merges and scope

Water and varied shorelines are part of the successful landscape direction. A bridge is an approved **option**, not a requirement. Validated one-unit wooden spans are implemented and approved in A2. A proposed crossing needs explicit deck/support geometry and continuous supported travel while preserving the water; no silent dry-terrain substitution. Bridge-deck defence placement, water combat and destructibility are unapproved. Where a dry bank is constrained, show the trade-off honestly rather than claiming a water-crossing sketch is already buildable.

Selected fixed merges are approved and their route engine exists. They remain optional in later carving, with no per-zone or level-number quota. A useful merge retains branch-specific opportunities, shared coverage and a later catch without making the junction the sole sensible investment. Route topology and entrance timing are separate. Do not multiply the finite roster or invent a five-wave schedule to make a sketch work. Independent exits, dynamic routing, player-built mazes and deployment/economy/outcome rules require their own decisions.

A1–A4 now have carving authorisation. The remaining twelve levels stay populated and path-free. Separately, the latest user request authorises two base drones and existing enemies only in A1 after A4 carving. This workflow does not authorise combat elsewhere.

## Critter connectivity after carving

**Critter placement rule (18 October):** a collision-free spawn point is not enough. Ground critters must start in connected, usable roaming ground with several meaningful destinations and room to flee. Reject isolated single-node pockets and tiny scenery cages. Check the actual movement/navigation envelope after carving or placement changes, then observe completed journeys; choose a better seed instead of trapping wildlife among props. Keep birds on supported perches with clear departure/arrival flight. This is an authoring/readability requirement, not a new confinement radius or gameplay mechanic.

Run `bun scripts/wildlife-roaming-audit.js LEVEL_ID` on changed previews. The offline probe reports component size/reach and completed movement; its two-box reach/one-box travel checks are authoring diagnostics, not new wildlife gameplay limits. A4’s original isolated seed is retained in the historical proposal so the regression test proves it fails while corrected seeds pass.
