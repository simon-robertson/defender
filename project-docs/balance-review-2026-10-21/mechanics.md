Gun/Arc burst periods are `(pulses−1)*gap + cooldown`; missile magazine periods are `(rounds−1)*gap + reload`, giving 5/7.5/10 seconds. Reload is not added after a second one-second cooldown. A fully loaded magazine also has substantial first-contact burst value that a sustained average hides. [WeaponCycle](../../engine/weapon.js) permits at most one current live pulse per step; interrupted bursts cancel and cooldown runs from the last actual pulse. Target death, loss of visibility, range exit and aim changes therefore alter achieved cadence. The fixed 120 Hz simulation adds small timing quantisation; these figures are nominal arithmetic, not measured effective damage per second.

The gate sequence is gun base/I from wave 1; gun II and Arc base/I from wave 5 (A2); Arc II and missile base/I from wave 9 (A3); missile II from wave 13 (A4). Generator base is offered from wave 1 when affordable, but the initial two-token wallet cannot purchase its four-token item immediately.

## Chain and generator composition

[ArcSpirePreview](../../game/arc-spire-preview.js) gives each useful primary pulse from either upgraded tier at most one secondary hit. Secondary strength is 25% of the **full effective primary strength**, even when the primary has less remaining HP. A lethal primary can conduct through its frozen final pose. Unboosted secondary damage is 12 for Arc I and 16.25 for Arc II; boosted values are 15 and 20.3125. There is no recursion or speculative secondary promise in the planner.

Primary admission uses inclusive horizontal defence-centre to enemy-root distance. Chain admission instead uses a three-dimensional distance: enemy-root XZ plus their current `aimY` difference, limited to half the originating Arc range, hence 42/54 units in play. The recipient may lie outside the spire's own targeting circle. It must be another active living enemy with a finite aim height and a physically connected outgoing/arrival surface pair. Scenery and live defence solids can block the link. Airborne recipients are supported; they receive no blanket immunity.

Eligible recipients undergo up to sixteen outgoing/arrival surface attempts, with the surface sampler itself bounded to sixteen attempts. A reservoir choice treats the successfully sampled recipients uniformly. This is not a guarantee that every geometrically possible clear neighbour passes the finite stochastic surface test. Primary centre visibility can also succeed while the sampled surface fails; such a target is temporarily excluded for .5 seconds (`surfaceRejects`). Counting only range neighbours overestimates real chain opportunities.

[EnhancerPreview](../../game/enhancer-preview.js) gives exactly +25% strength to valid weapons in the four edge-sharing squares on supported dry ground at path height. Diagonals do not qualify. Multiple generators take the maximum bonus, rather than adding or multiplying it. Original strengths are retained, so resets cannot compound it. The bonus affects both Arc primary and secondary once: `baseStrength * 1.25 * .25` for the secondary. Missiles copy effective strength into the flight slot at launch; contact and incoming-damage claims use that value once. Fire rate, cooldown, range and chain radius do not change.

A generator has a four-token opportunity cost and occupies a real court/solid that can obstruct other weapons. Its nominal marginal contribution is `.25 * sum(recipient output)`. Four identical supported weapons gain the output of a fifth identical one; one recipient gains only a quarter weapon. For example, four Gun II recipients add 47.297 nominal DPS, whereas a separate Gun II costs three tokens rather than four; four Missile II recipients add 216 nominal DPS, whereas another Missile II costs nine. These comparisons omit legal space, coverage, overkill, flight losses and pool contention and therefore are hypotheses for placement-aware comparison, not price recommendations.

## Traffic, air and projectile limits

Spawn spacing is part of chain balance. Along a straight route with equal-speed actors, centre separation is approximately speed times release gap. A2's 14-unit/s ground traffic at 2.8-second gaps gives 39.2 units, within Arc I's 42-unit sphere before aim-height differences. A4's 15*2.8 gives exactly 42. Common B ground spacing is 10*3.6 = 36. Common C ground spacing is 14*3.5 = 49 and D ground spacing is 12*3.6 = 43.2: those fit Arc II's 54 but exceed Arc I's 42. Common C air spacing is 22*3 = 66, while D air is 16*3 = 48. Route bends, merging approaches, mixed heights and actual moving poses alter these straight-line estimates; use current spatial opportunities rather than release gaps alone. A3/B3 use deliberately offset branches, so each branch's nominal gap does not describe shared-tail traffic.

Gun anti-air ability requires the actual ±35° pitch envelope, angular acquisition and both muzzle-to-torso rays clear. A close aircraft can be inside horizontal range but above the gun's reachable elevation; silhouettes, geometry and scenery matter. Air species use play altitude 44, with their actual articulated torso heights. All air actors are excluded from route-centre future-coverage promises, but remain eligible for live acquisition. Camera visibility is not combat visibility. Arc has a fixed elevated crown and no gun elevation gate, but still needs visibility and a sampled exposed side surface. Missile admission has no straight LOS requirement; it checks a forecast of the real curved corridor, including launcher self-exclusion and other defence solids.

Missiles have unchanged .5-second boost at 64 units/s, .4-second coast, then 180-unit/s attack speed with 630-unit/s² acceleration and existing angular steering/terminal cutoff. They have eight-second maximum lifetime and swept target/scenery collision. A single projectile damages only its assigned enemy; its explosion is not enemy splash. Death can orphan a round toward its last known point rather than transfer its damage to another enemy. Forecast admission does not certify a hit against subsequent turning/pose changes.

The fixed pool has 36 actors shared between **loaded** and flying rounds. Deferred magazines rotate allocation to avoid simple monopolisation, but large missile arsenals can wait for slots, have partial magazines and refill delays. A large nominal launcher total is therefore not independent per-unit DPS. Record `poolDeferrals`, ammo/reloading time, launches, contacts, collisions, passes and orphaning. Some missile outcome counters describe overlapping events over a flight's lifetime; they are not necessarily exclusive terminal categories. Missile impacts can also displace/brace surviving enemies without reducing their route-distance speed; this changes geometry and disables their temporary route-coverage forecasts.

## Economy and historical witness

[PlaySession](../../play/session.js) starts with two tokens and awards two when a fully resolved wave settles without reaching the ten-miss defeat limit. A wave with one or more escapes can still earn its two tokens. Deliberately accepting misses can finance later purchases; that is an existing risk/reward policy, not unlimited income. There is no per-kill bounty, extra level award, sell refund or recall income. Ownership, tiers, wallet and misses persist; positions return to inventory on a level transfer. Purchases and upgrades require loadout; deployment/recall require deploy preparation. Each transaction and wave index is validated.

Before encounter n, the cumulative earned budget is `2 + 2*(n−1)`; before the final encounter it is 128, while a completed 64-wave run has been awarded 130 in total. Final wallet is therefore `130 − lifetime spend`, not `128 − lifetime spend`. Aggressively spending every affordable token proves affordability, not that all purchases were necessary.

The [historical 16:09 UTC mixed witness](../engine-optimisation-2026-10-21/mixed-campaign.json) completes 64 waves/1,084 enemies with zero escapes, 126 spent and four tokens left. Its end roster is seven Gun II (21 tokens), eight Arc II (48), six Missile II plus one base missile (57): 22 owned and 22 placed. End-of-zone cumulative spend is A31/B63/C96/D126, so continued investment is built into its policy. It contains no generator purchase. Current hashes differ for config, session, engine, placement and shared missile flight; this is historical evidence, not a fresh proof of current balance. Its useful family damage sums exactly to all scheduled HP in each of its 64 waves, as checked against current unchanged enemy HP schedules in a bounded read-only calculation.

The old policy always upgrades its first eligible owned unit before buying its next repeated family. Placement uses hand-picked preferred courts before a centre-distance route-count fallback; it does not optimise actual LOS, pitch, missile flight or generator adjacency. It can legally own units it cannot deploy. No claim that 126 tokens is a minimum, that the policy is optimal, or that aggregate damage identifies the best purchase follows from that witness.

## Evidence traps and defensible comparisons

1. **Confirmed report-only lifecycle bug:** [campaign-balance-audit.js](../../scripts/campaign-balance-audit.js) constructs `undeployed` after the completion callback has called `session.finishWave()`. At a level boundary that clears every `unit.box`, so all owned units are falsely listed as undeployed despite the recorded placement array containing them. Record deployment state before combat, or derive reserves as owned IDs absent from the arrangement. Production deployment is unaffected.
2. Its family `onHit` wrapping **does include both Arc channels**: each calls the same `this.onHit`, and the original callback returns useful HP removed. Missile wrapping on `pool.onHit` similarly measures actual contact damage. The wrappers are installed after `startWave()` rebuilds controllers, and fresh controllers prevent cross-wave wrapper accumulation. Existing family totals cannot separately identify primary and secondary or individual unit contribution. Track secondary context by wrapping `chain()` and the controller callback while preserving returns and ordering.
3. `arcShots` counts primary pulses, not secondary hits. Damage share is an allocation under one common target/planner state; removing one family can cause others to replace its damage. Family damage per lifetime token is not an isolated causal efficiency estimate. Overkill should be recorded as attempted minus useful damage; all-cleared useful damage is capped at the scheduled HP total.
4. Replacing `arcs.chain` with a no-op removes random draws from the same xorshift stream used by later **primary** contact sampling. A no-chain outcome then combines lost secondary damage with different later surface samples. For a closer ablation, execute original chain sampling and suppress only its secondary damage callback using an explicit context flag. This preserves draws until health-dependent state diverges; changed health then legitimately changes targets/candidates and later RNG use. Do not describe the resulting playthrough as identical random realizations. Current realized secondary damage and opportunity counts are the strongest direct mechanism evidence; use ablation as a scenario result with this qualification.
5. Paired comparisons should preserve earned budget, transaction legality, available tiers, placements/order, simulation step and initial seeds. Include both a fixed placement witness and a placement-aware alternative when judging generator value or family dominance. Record outstanding HP, escapes, late interception margin, unused budget, reserves, actual coverage and flight/pool losses. Equal DPS circles alone cannot decide campaign difficulty.

## Confirmed Arc retry bookkeeping issue

There is also a source-level gameplay defect separate from the balance recommendations. Arc's `surfaceRetryAt` array is described as a fixed roster slot cache, but reads/writes use `this.targets.indexOf(target)`. [PlayCombat](../../play/engine.js) replaces that array with `enemies.activeTargets`, and [PlayEnemies.resolve](../../play/enemies.js) compacts it with `splice` whenever an enemy dies or escapes. A rejected enemy's retry timestamp can therefore move to a different living enemy, while the rejected one loses its intended wait. Spawns appended into a vacated position can inherit a previous actor's timestamp too.

A bounded read-only invocation of the actual `ArcSpirePreview.prototype.eligible` method reproduced the ownership error without loading a world: targets A/B/C, time zero, retry timestamp .5 at B's current index produced eligibility A=true/B=false/C=true. Removing A then produced B=true/C=false. This is not a full combat reproduction and establishes no measured escape impact, but directly demonstrates index ownership is unstable. The potential misattributed delay is bounded by the remaining .5-second rejection interval and can affect achieved Arc primary/chain opportunities. No fix was made; the balance runner should retain current behavior and the report should distinguish this issue from a recommendation to adjust prices or damage.

No token duplication exploit was established. Balance changes remain recommendations for user review. The design document's older no-chain/single-target generalisations should be read alongside its current upgraded-Arc description; this note proposes no independent documentation or runtime patch.
