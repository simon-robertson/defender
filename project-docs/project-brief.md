## Units and boxes

**24 × 24 × 24 is the final agreed box size**, superseding the earlier 15-unit and 32-unit decisions.

- A single grid cell measures **1 unit** along each axis.
- The larger placement grid consists of **boxes**, each measuring **24 × 24 × 24 units (XYZ)**. Boxes are used to place models, pathways, and other level elements.
- A model's **width and length must each be no greater than 24 units**, so its footprint fits within one box. Its **height may exceed 24 units**, extending above one box.

## Engineering questions for the design stage

These questions guide later proposals; they do not select an architecture.

- How should quad geometry, per-part transforms, and rigs be stored so static geometry can be reused while parts animate?
- Which geometry, transform, and instance data can remain on the GPU, and what actually needs updating each frame?
- How should the world be divided for visibility, simulation, and GPU submission as the camera pans across large levels?
- Which transient objects need pools, how are they reset, and what happens at capacity?
- How should simulation timing relate to rendering and future audio events without adding an audio subsystem now?
- What render-pass and resource boundaries will allow approved effects to be introduced later without committing to their cost today?

Native GPU render targets and depth buffers are compatible with texture-free models. The user explicitly permits creating textures for visual effects when needed; this does not change the model material style or authorise unrelated effects. A model's authored axis alignment must also be distinguished from its transformed orientation during play.
