# Dynamic drone obstacle envelopes

18 October 2026. Engineer implementation supporting the user's predictive positioning and collision-avoidance request. Main flight integration and final browser verification are owned by Lead and the combat Engineer. No model, layout, combat timing, wildlife behaviour or renderer resources change in this module.

## Ownership and API

`engine/drone-obstacles.js` exports `DroneObstacles(world, enemies)` and `DRONE_OBSTACLE_PREDICTION_SECONDS = 1`. Its scene capacity comes directly from loaded actor rigs and non-drone defence rigs. Every model part's local voxel AABB is cached once by shared part identity. Each live instance's current world matrix transforms that AABB once in `update(dt)`; queries reuse these arrays and do not inspect voxels or sample paths.

- `setWildlife(states)` binds the existing wildlife states after construction; unknown scene IDs fail explicitly rather than expanding capacity. It refreshes the initial pose snapshot.
- `update(dt)` runs after enemy/wildlife movement and before drone planning/movement. Other defences may articulate later in the frame: predicted checks cover their complete possible yaw/pitch/recoil envelope.
- `clearBox(bounds, predictSeconds = 0)`, `clearSegment(ax, ay, az, bx, by, bz, radius = 16.5, predictSeconds = 0)` and `pointClear(...)` return clearance. `segmentClear` aliases `clearSegment` for integration convenience. Queries allocate no arrays or objects. Actor AABBs reject distant sources before current-part tests.
- `clearTimedSegment(ax, ay, az, bx, by, bz, radius, startSeconds, endSeconds, halfHeight = radius)` compares one short aircraft segment with obstacle movement over that same future interval. Times clamp to the cached one-second horizon. Neutral pad travel can use X/Z radius 11 with half-height 5. The query reuses cached unshifted articulation envelopes and scratch arrays; it does not transform matrices or inspect voxels again. Flight integration certifies an escape using contiguous short intervals and checks the next second again each frame.
- Zero prediction tests the current posed actor parts. Fixed defences use their full motion envelope even at zero prediction, covering defence articulation later in the same frame. **Any positive requested horizon uses the entire cached one-second envelope**, conservatively covering shorter horizons. Requests longer than one second remain bounded at one second; they do not promise clearance for an entire distant journey.
- `reset()` drops observed-motion history and refreshes current poses. `dispose()` clears source/state/matrix references and releases every typed array. It is idempotent. `inspect()` creates an on-demand diagnostic object; it is not a simulation hot path.

Living enemy parts, ground wildlife, perched/flying birds and non-drone defence parts are included. Death is checked both at refresh and query, so a just-killed enemy disappears immediately. Drone bodies use the combat Engineer's fleet checks; their stationary pads, terrain and scenery use the existing navigation obstacles. Weather, particles, smoke, debris, missile projectiles and other cosmetic effects are excluded. Launcher magazine wells reserve the maximum seated-round tip envelope, including while empty; individual guided-missile actors are never collision sources. Arc/generator/silo source parts have no runtime articulation; turret yaw, pitch and recoil are the existing moving solid defence parts.

## Conservative prediction

Current bounds include full root and parent articulation, including posed wings, legs, gun pitch/yaw and recoil. Each rigid part's transformed AABB can contain empty corners of irregular geometry; these are safe false positives rather than voxel-exact contact.

For future queries, a joint-chain envelope encloses all rotations about the authored pivots. This intentionally allows more articulation than the actual mechanical limits and is larger than the current pose. Fixed defence parts retain their authored boxes; turret joints include the approved 1.25-unit recoil. Wildlife uses its tighter existing complete-wing/body radius and height contract.

Enemy translation follows its actual complete route, including every intervening 24-unit centre-line corner and its endpoint. The exit-side forecast stops at exit. If the preview will wrap inside the one-second horizon, a **separate preallocated entrance envelope** covers the root's reappearance and remaining entrance-side route travel. The two regions are never joined into a whole-board teleport sweep. Exceptionally short routes that wrap repeatedly inside one second reserve their complete route in the entrance envelope. This collision-only forecast does not change attack prediction or promise shots beyond the enemy's current exit. Active impact recovery also includes the existing maximum displacement/bob contracts. Flying birds follow their existing eased X/Z journey and cubic Y flight, including exact vertical extrema and the currently active fright speed increase. Moving ground wildlife gets a conservative reachable disk over its current movement speed, rather than an extrapolated direction that would miss the next navigation turn. Its low hop is included without blocking safe overhead flight. Custom moving actors without a known route use observed translation capped at 96 units/second; lap changes, reset and faster discontinuities discard that history.

This is a local navigation safety forecast, not a general physical collision simulation. It does not predict future random wildlife departure decisions, future scares, later enemy hits or arbitrary changes to an actor's route. The next frame refreshes changed states. Ordinary positive-horizon checks are deliberately spatial unions over the whole prediction window; they can block a place that an actor will have vacated before arrival. Time-matched escape queries instead use just the selected route/bird interval, including every route corner and exact bird vertical extremum. They retain separate exit/entrance regions during wraps. Currently frightened birds conservatively cover normal through boosted future phases so an unobserved fright expiry cannot leave a falsely clear region behind the forecast. Ground wildlife retains its conservative reachable disk because future navigation choices are not fixed. Each short interval still uses a spatial union, so false positives remain possible. The flight integration must check current motion, braking and imminent intrusion into a stationary drone, and select an escape route when needed. Holding alone cannot avoid an incoming flyer.

## Resources and verification

Actual A1 contains **9 dynamic obstacle actors and 43 rigid parts**, using **6,086 bytes of retained typed storage** plus bounded source records and matrix references. This includes 49 bytes per actor for the independent entrance-wrap envelope/active flag, 72 bytes per actor for the time-query base envelope/observed velocity, and 48 shared bytes for its interval scratch box. No GPU buffers, draw calls, shaders or effects are added. The module does not retain the world or any voxel arrays after construction; existing shared matrix arrays remain scene-owned. Local geometry cache construction is scene-only. Queries and updates allocate no recurring arrays or objects. No frame-time or GPU performance claim is made.

`bun test tests/drone-obstacles.test.js` passes **13 tests / 2,862 assertions**. Checks cover a predicted airborne crossing, low ground animal/overhead clearance, current articulated wing/leg poses, a route corner and exit boundary, separate entrance-wrap prediction without a false teleport corridor (including repeated short-route wraps, dead removal and reused storage), exact bird flight peak/full wing envelope, time-matched upward escape crossing, a previously occupied origin becoming clear in a later interval, fright-expiry uncertainty, time-matched route corners/wraps, neutral half-height, later-in-frame defence yaw/pitch/recoil (including zero-horizon queries), seated launcher tips with launched-missile exclusion, lap/reset discontinuities, unknown capacity rejection and teardown. A real A1 test advances enemies and wildlife for 20 seconds and checks sampled occupied model-cell centres throughout posed flight, turns and hops.
