Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: one polished landscape concept-art approval sheet for the Defender tower defence game.
Primary request: a new stationary MISSILE LAUNCHER. Its missiles launch VERTICALLY UP from the TOP of the defence, clear the unit, then curve toward and automatically track their assigned enemy. Present a coherent BASE, UPGRADE 1, UPGRADE 2 model family in the established Moss & Porcelain art direction. This is visual design approval only, not a production asset or real-world engineering blueprint.

Composition: wide landscape 3:2 sheet, warm ivory studio backdrop, quiet refined editorial layout. Three complete large launcher designs across the main row, consistent physical scale and identical elevated three-quarter view, clear top decks, a common ground baseline and equal square mounting-pad footprint. Base left, upgrade 1 centre, upgrade 2 right. Generous spacing, nothing cropped. Main designs take about seventy percent of the sheet. Small supporting studies along the bottom: a missile design on the left, a simple flight-path annotation in the middle, and tiny play-scale thumbnails of the three launcher silhouettes on the right. These supporting studies must be subordinate, airy and simple.

Shared launcher design: a low, broad vertical-launch battery, sturdy and charming but powerful, unmistakably distinct from a conventional forward-firing gun turret or tall electrical tower. A layered warm stone square mounting pad supports a short dark mechanical plinth and a substantial porcelain armoured body. Broad cream corner shoulders surround a dark forest-green HORIZONTAL TOP DECK with deep square launch openings pointing STRAIGHT UP. Each square opening has a chunky clean dark rim and a recessed visible compact missile nose; the missiles are seated inside the unit. Moss-green rim braces and restrained geometric service panels separate the cream armour. A small vermilion rectangular identity mark on the front. Clean orthogonal steps and solid engineered connections, no delicate dangling parts. No turret yaw bearing and no angled launcher rack: the entire battery stands fixed and upright. Leave every top aperture unobstructed; no lid or armour covers the launch direction.

Upgrade progression, compact within the same square ground footprint:
BASE: TWO square top launch cells, side by side. Short, complete and capable, compact porcelain shoulders, simple moss rim braces and a single front vermilion marker.
UPGRADE 1: FOUR square top launch cells in an unmistakable 2-by-2 array. Slightly taller, reinforced corner shoulders, stronger moss braces, tidy recessed dark service slots and a more substantial stepped body.
UPGRADE 2: SIX square top launch cells in an unmistakable 2-by-3 array. The strongest taller stepped body, reinforced corners and a compact rear service block below the launch deck so the openings remain clear. Retain restrained massing, not an oversized fortress. Do not add other weapon systems, dishes or towers.
The future model must fit within a 24-by-24-unit square footprint; target a roughly 22-by-22 occupied envelope without printing dimensions. Height may grow. Launch-cell counts are design motifs only, not statements about burst count, stored ammunition or gameplay balance.

Missile supporting study: one small stylised missile, isolated and upright. Slender porcelain rectangular-section body, simple stepped square nose, narrow dark green collar, one vermilion band, and four SHORT rectilinear moss tail fins. All surfaces are broad flat planes with orthogonal stepped geometry, visually buildable from unit cubes. Tail-fin span fits a launch cell. Show the whole missile, recognisable but toy-like game machinery rather than realistic military equipment. No exposed internal components or cutaway.

Flight-path supporting study: a thin muted green diagram line rises straight upward out of a tiny launcher outline, then bends into a gentle descending guided curve ending at a small neutral target marker. Small arrowheads communicate direction. One tiny upright missile at the initial vertical part and a second tiny tilted missile along the curved part are successive positions of the same missile, clearly diagrammatic. No explosion, contrail or exhaust. Caption this study "LAUNCH > TRACK > HIT". Diagram lines are graphic annotations, not in-game effects.
The small play-scale study shows the three completed launcher shapes at miniature game-view size, supporting silhouette comparison.

Palette: dominant warm porcelain #F1E8D5, deep forest mechanisms #243E35, moss green secondary panels #738B55, warm stone mounting pads #D9CCAA, sparse vermilion identity accents #D65D45. Consistent palette balance on all three variants. Tints and shades express planar faces and deep cell recesses. Matte, texture-free surfaces, broad clean colour blocking. No glossy metal, camouflage, weathering, grunge, scratches, busy decals, coloured global tint or neon.

Style and lighting: premium stylised game concept illustration with sculptural digital rendering, clear planar daylight and subtle ground contact shadows. Crisp chunky shapes, sparse meaningful seams, strong silhouettes readable at small game scale. Axis-aligned rectilinear construction; minimal illustrative edge softening. Not a photorealistic weapon render and not a noisy military kitbash.

Text, exact and restrained: heading "MOSS & PORCELAIN"; subheading "VERTICAL MISSILE LAUNCHER"; beneath the main models "BASE", "UPGRADE 1", "UPGRADE 2". Small lower study labels "MISSILE", "LAUNCH > TRACK > HIT", "PLAY SCALE". Dark forest typography with no other prose.
Avoid: people, scenery, actual enemy models, wheels, tracks, legs, forward gun barrels, sideways or angled launch cells, vertical stacks of ports on front walls, domes, rotating radar, lasers, lightning, shields, extra weapons, floating armour, round tubes, hoses, realistic engineering detail, measurements, statistics, ammunition counts in text, smoke, fog, haze, explosions, fire, glowing armour, bloom, lens flare, logos or watermarks.
