## Current cooperative planner (17–18 October 2026)

`engine/target-planner.js` supplies one round-robin ranking pass per rendered frame. The remaining guns only validate their existing locks. Aim, recoil, cooldown and actual paired-muzzle range/LOS checks still run every frame; invalid or dead targets stop firing immediately and replacement acquisition waits at most one planner rotation. C now has the approved Tripod/Ray roster and uses the same planner.

Each enemy has a positive `threat` stat (provisional **1** for all ten enemy models). Urgency is threat divided by seconds to the current route exit, floored at one second. Uncovered enemies rank ahead of adequately covered enemies; within a 10% urgency band, fewer pulses to kill, route progress, distance and stable ID break ties. Keep a lock for at least 0.35 seconds and finish a still-valid burst; then switch when the current enemy is covered or a challenger is at least 25% more urgent. These are provisional planner settings, not new weapon balance.

At scene load, sample actual paired-muzzle visibility/range at one-unit route increments and both recoil endpoints, trimming each continuous coverage interval by one unit at either end. Forecast only the next continuous window before this lap's exit, including actual cooldown, remaining burst pulses and a 0.75-second acquisition allowance. Count one damage pulse for both barrels. Require **125% of health not already claimed by launched missiles** from other committed guns before treating an enemy as covered. This sampled forecast is an estimate, not guaranteed future damage. Actors whose aim point is off the route centre, or which bank in flight, explicitly disable these speculative probes/credits. Actual live acquisition, shots and launched missile claims remain valid. This includes the original Spider’s half-unit forward offset.

An idle downstream defence may accept one explicit reservation when an upstream defence has another useful target. Busy guns count only while actually committed to this enemy in their current window; a stale lock cannot promise a later return. When a gun relies on others' damage, reserve those owners to that enemy. Each owner promises capacity to only one enemy and cannot freely abandon it. Guns and Arc Spires keep shooting when there is no alternative, even when coverage is sufficient; silos instead wait once actual launched missiles cover the remaining health. Death, route departure, a preview lap reset, disposal, exhausted capacity and full incoming-missile coverage invalidate gun reservations. All actual shots remain independently checked.

Runtime uses reused scratch arrays and precomputed numeric intervals; no route geometry scan or allocation happens during normal thinking. Work is bounded by active defences × eligible enemies × their short coverage-window lists, plus fixed flight-slot queries for incoming damage, with only one defence ranking per frame. Larger rosters grow scratch storage only on roster changes; newly added unprofiled actors receive ordinary threat targeting but no speculative gun damage credit. Static scenery assumptions match the existing visibility tree. Future projectile volleys receive no forecast credit; actual launched missiles use their separate flight records.

Focused planner checks cover threat/progress/health ranking, conservative delegation, ownership, cooldown and blocked/zero-strength rejection, lap/gap invalidation, hysteresis, exact pulse counts, fairness, disposal, roster growth and non-thinking guns rejecting invalid shots. ## Incoming missile damage (18 October 2026)

The user asked silos to avoid launching more missiles once the ones already assigned could destroy an enemy. `TargetPlanner` accepts an optional `incomingDamage(target)` callback, defaulting to zero. `DefencePreview` supplies `MissilePreview.incomingDamage(target)`. The flight pool owns the bounded accounting: only actual launched, still-valid missiles for the same living enemy object and lap count; loaded rounds and future magazine shots do not. The querying silo's own flights count alongside other silos. Flight retirement, known misses, death/orphaning, lap or roster invalidation, stalls, reset and disposal remove the corresponding claim; physical orphan or missed flight may continue independently.

Let **H** be current health, **P** the assigned in-flight damage and **F** other committed guns' speculative capacity. The planner computes **residual = max(0, H − P)** and treats the enemy as covered when residual is zero or **F ≥ 1.25 × residual**. `coverageDamage()` and `predictedCoverage` remain gun forecasts only; they never include P. This credits each missile once and applies the safety margin only to speculative gun damage. For example, 20 missile damage against 100 HP leaves an 80-HP residual, requiring a 100-damage gun forecast. A 19-damage claim requires 101.25, so that same gun cannot accept the delegation. Unknown or invalid health remains unbounded rather than inventing a lethal threshold; nonfinite or negative incoming values contribute zero.

Recruitment uses this same residual. Full missile coverage releases an existing gun reservation and cannot create another one. Partial coverage still retains the required gun owners. The existing threat/exit ranking, actual-health tie break, 0.35-second hold and valid-burst rules are unchanged for guns and Arc Spires. Missile and Arc `capacity()` remain zero because their route probes cannot certify future attacks. Silo launch eligibility supplies the stricter current-health-versus-incoming check, including a live check before each launch; it does not add another candidate-ranking pass. No speculative gun promise by itself imposes that hard silo wait.

Each incoming query scans at most the existing **36 flight slots**, with no new maps, per-missile objects or route/contact probes. Planner queries happen during its existing candidate scan and reservation checks; its scratch arrays and capacities are unchanged. The callback is released on planner disposal. This is a bounded work/storage description, not a measured frame-rate claim. The lead owns combined flight/controller integration and production review.

Fresh focused verification: `bun test tests/target-planner.test.js` passes **24 tests and 226 assertions**. Four new checks cover residual-margin boundaries and exact-once credit, release/avoidance of unnecessary gun reservations, unknown-health/invalid-claim safety and mixed-family one-thinker behavior with zero Arc/missile forecasts. The existing checks continue to cover the default zero-incoming policy, live-shot validation, target hold/burst rules and reservation lifecycle. Flight and integration checks are owned by Engineer and lead respectively and are not included in this count.

## Projectile leading

The user approved leading shots for projectile-based defences. `engine/targeting.js` now dispatches by attack delivery: current gun rules stay `delivery: 'hitscan', projectile: null` and aim at the present target centre. A future projectile rule must explicitly set `delivery: 'projectile'` and `projectile.speedUnitsPerSecond` to a finite positive number. No projectile weapon or speed has been selected for the live preview.

`DefenceAim.solve(originX, originZ, target)` returns eligibility and reuses its `point` array containing aim X, aim Z and travel seconds. `engine/aim.js` solves the earliest non-negative constant-velocity intercept. Lead therefore scales with target movement and projectile travel time; faster projectiles need less lead. Both the current target and predicted impact must be within maximum range. The prediction horizon is range divided by projectile speed. Missing velocity or an unreachable intercept makes that candidate ineligible, allowing the existing selection policy to choose another target; it never silently substitutes an unled shot.

Dogs expose `velocityX` and `velocityZ` in units per second from their current path segment. This follows actual travel direction, independently of their smoothly turning visual heading. A preview-loop reset cannot create a velocity spike. Path samples, aim storage and actor objects are reused; scans preserve the selected candidate's solution even after evaluating other candidates.

This is horizontal prediction from a stationary firing origin, currently the yaw pivot. It assumes unchanged target velocity during flight; it does not anticipate future path bends, acceleration or preview teleports. It adds no arbitrary offset toward the model's nose. A real projectile weapon will still need its own approved launch origin, vertical aiming and travel/hit behaviour. This support does not alter the current hitscan turret animation or add projectile simulation.

Derived horizon and squared-impact comparisons allow only a small machine-precision tolerance so exact diagonal range boundaries are not lost to floating-point rounding. Accepted travel time remains capped at the horizon. Tests also reject genuinely outside-range intercepts; this is not gameplay range padding.
