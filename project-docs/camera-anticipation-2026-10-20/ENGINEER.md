## Implemented behaviour

Both camera controllers share a read-only `CameraAnticipation` helper. It predicts a nearby future encounter between an enemy and an idle gun, Arc Spire or missile launcher. It samples each enemy's actual complete `PathMotion`, including bends, route choice and shared tails, instead of extrapolating a velocity across corners. Current and scheduled enemies can supply a future opportunity. The helper reads real `maxRange`, weapon cooldown/burst timing, missile ammunition/reload state, approximate actual source height, gun pitch bounds and required source-to-future-target line of sight. Unknown families, missing route/speed/height, unusable ranges, explicitly nonpositive/invalid strength, and unavailable missile magazines are skipped conservatively.

These are filming opportunities, not attack promises. The helper never invokes combat eligibility/planning/contact methods, which have mutable scratch/counter/reservation effects; it never changes targeting, damage claims, poses, flight routes, health, readiness or gameplay. It does not predict enemy survival, exact muzzle articulation, Arc surface contact, missile-pool admission or future target competition. Approximate LOS/pitch checks can differ from the eventual physical shot.

A 0.6-second editorial scan tests at most 96 enemy/defence pairs, rotating across larger finite rosters, and at most 37 half-second route samples per pair over an 18-second horizon. The lead window depends on camera-to-engagement travel at the operator/drone speed and desired stand-off, plus setup time. A selected enemy/defence event point is committed for four seconds, rather than chasing a freshly shifted goal each frame. Current recent real shots or an ongoing visible burst take priority. Ending a forecast releases its camera subject so ordinary real action selection can resume without the old six/seven-second subject hold delaying it.

Anticipated composition includes the future enemy position and the defence. It replaces the live actor aim point while the forecast is active and invalidates an obsolete movement goal, allowing existing navigation/shot-quality tests to preposition for that location. The camera prepositions when its current station cannot film the future event usefully. A useful station is retained: Cam man can stand still, while Drone now keeps a small continuous survey drift around its stable framing anchor. Arming, saved overview restoration, scene release and wave changes retain their previous lifecycle. The latest Drone motion pass below now continues gentle translation during settling as well as combat; pause still freezes it exactly. Helper state resets on leave, refresh and bind, preventing stale predictions from overriding late settling explosions.

## Final continuous-motion and zoom revision

The user subsequently requests smoother translation and gimbal easing, an active drone that keeps moving, and 20% less maximum magnification. The drone now surveys a six-unit-radius circle around a stable shot anchor at a nominal 0.65 units/s while retaining/panning toward the action. It continues this gentle drift during settling. This is local motion around a useful shot, not a high-speed timed lap around the enemy. Slow arrival within five units captures the framing anchor and explicitly transitions from route travel to drift; this avoids endlessly circling an unfinished nominal waypoint. Departure/approach directions ease into the existing velocity instead of resetting speed at every goal. Existing swept and airborne-floor gates still validate actual movement. If an authored packed scene offers no safe direction, collision safety can interrupt translation; no such interruption appears in the current campaign witnesses.

Translation uses bounded three-dimensional acceleration and acceleration change. Soft descent guidance slows before the flight floor, avoiding an ordinary hard-clamp landing. Yaw, pitch and FOV have damped rate targets, with maximum rates 1.1 rad/s, 0.7 rad/s and 9°/s respectively; their acceleration bounds are 1.8 rad/s², 1.2 rad/s² and 12°/s². The finite-difference test measures actual positions and orientation/FOV changes through a clear three-leg journey and the transition into drift, rather than trusting internal velocity fields. Its maximum measured translational acceleration is 24.000000001 units/s² and acceleration change 48.00000009 units/s³; no blocks occur, and 3,349 of 4,800 integration steps explicitly drift. These are CPU motion observations, not hardware/FPS measurements.

Minimum drone FOV is now `2 * atan(tan(42° / 2) / 0.8)` = **51.26618197°**, with maximum FOV still 76°. This reduces maximum optical magnification to exactly 80% of its prior value. It is not an arithmetic 20% increase of the degree value. Optical-axis depth-of-field focus remains active independently of the zoom easing.

The camera integrates elapsed simulation time in substeps no larger than 1/120 second, including each render frame's final fraction. Previously a fractional enemy-exit frame could leave a nonzero drone velocity but show zero actual displacement until settling; the new regression exposed and closes that one-frame stop. Pause and zero simulation elapsed time still freeze all motion exactly; long render gaps retain the 0.25-second camera travel cap.
