## Ownership and API

`shared/arc-effects.js` owns bounded visual tuning; `engine/arc-effects.js` owns reusable CPU pools. The app creates its first `ArcEffects` only when an Arc level is activated and retains that pool for reuse. Its clock receives the same delta as enemies and weapon cycles. Call `advance(deltaSeconds)`, emit accepted hits during combat, then `prepare()` after animated poses are current. The renderer consumes the prepared buffers without advancing the simulation.

```js
effects.emit(sourceXYZ, impactXYZ, outwardNormalXYZ, at,
             attachmentMatrix = null, attachmentLocalPoint = null,
             isChain = false, sourceMatrix = null, sourceLocalPoint = null);
effects.reset(preserveTime = false);
renderer.setArcEffects(effects); // after setWorld; null in turret-only levels
effects.setWorld(world);        // after renderer binding; borrows world.visibility
```

Endpoints, normals and local points are copied. Optional arrival and departure rigid-part matrices are retained only until strike expiry, replacement or reset, so both contacts follow their enemy parts during a secondary strike. A lethal hit leaves its final pose stationary. Metal flecks start at the original contact and move independently. `setWorld` borrows only the existing static collision tree. `reset(true)` clears visuals and bone references while preserving the weapon clock and collider for R; ordinary reset/unload also clears the collider and resets time. Expired events are discarded rather than replayed after a stall.

Twelve strike slots are split into six primary and six secondary slots. Exhaustion replaces the oldest strike within its own bank; primary saturation cannot suppress every secondary beam. Each strike has exactly 32 main ribbon spans and no branches. Ninety-six metal-fleck slots emit six per hit; excess flecks are dropped. Counters expose replacements and drops. Shapes and random spark velocities are generated once per hit; all runtime storage is fixed. There are no per-shot or per-frame arrays, objects, typed-array views, timers or extra animation loops in this subsystem.

## Appearance and continuous lighting

The pale blue-white channel lasts 0.14 seconds. It starts hard, dips briefly, restrikes at 0.045 seconds with a small shape change, then collapses. The primary full fringe width starts at 1.8 units around a white core. Enemy-to-enemy channels use the same envelope at 70% width (1.26 units initially); both core and fringe, including the existing bloom source geometry, narrow together. Tube lighting and impact sparks retain their prior tuning. Unequal bends combine a coarse shape and finer transverse detail, with displacement up to roughly two units. The first four spans rise two units while moving four units outward from the exact terminal top, then the remaining channel descends toward the enemy. This keeps the primary launch connected and visible above its own terminal/crown. A secondary channel instead departs directly towards its recipient from an exposed enemy surface, using its first four spans for the first 12% of that transfer. No texture asset is used.

Six bright yellow-white flecks cool to orange. Their half-width is 0.13–0.26 units, initial outward normal speed 12–24 units/second, and added upward velocity 6–14 units/second. Provisional gravity is 90 units/second². Shared `sweepScenery` and `debrisSupport` query the existing exact terrain/scenery cuboids without a duplicate height map. Swept contacts prevent tunnelling; wall contacts deflect/dampen horizontal travel, while upward-facing support stops a fleck. The grounded fleck has a nonzero short tangent span just above its actual support and fades for 0.35 seconds. Flight has a three-second safety cap, with at most 120 integration substeps per active fleck per frame; ordinary substeps target 1/120 second. Full elapsed time counts toward the landing fade, including a stall. Water is not solid and flecks can land on the stream bed. The world zero-height plane handles empty/outside terrain. Flecks apply no damage, leave no scorch and create no new light or bloom pass.

`engine/arc-lighting.js` defines a distinct finite-segment light source. Each active strike packs eight spans following every fourth main-channel vertex, including the terminal escape. Each shading point projects onto the finite segment and uses the nearest point on that segment, including its end caps. Thus a surface beside the middle receives light even if both endpoints are out of range. This is not a series of point lights.

Main tube radius is 18 units, strength 4.8, and linear RGB is `[0.43, 0.66, 1]`, modulated by the strike's pulse envelope. The radial curve matches the established local-light falloff. The strongest span contribution within one strike is used, avoiding brighter tessellation joints; independent strikes add. This is an artistic tube-field approximation, not a physical line-radiance integral. The light uses a coarser polyline than the visible fine bends and remains unshadowed, like existing transient point lights, so nearby walls do not block spill.

Existing point/model/explosion lights remain in their original uniform and retain their arithmetic. The tube contribution is added through the same production material paths: one-sided diffuse for opaque geometry, water and debris; the existing oriented smoke response; droplet scattering for rain/snow; and two-sided response for leaves. There is no arc-specific brightening floor on materials or weather.

`engine/arc-shaders.js` draws camera-facing capsule spans in one instanced additive draw after ordinary display composition. Full-resolution opaque depth hides them and water attenuates submerged portions. Perspective segments are clipped at the near plane before widening. Only the electrical channel contributes to the existing reduced bloom emitter pass; impact sparks stay crisp. Existing blur/composite targets and passes are reused.

## Cost and lifetime

Measured typed-array storage per CPU pool is **39,644 bytes**, plus twelve arrival and twelve departure matrix-reference slots, one borrowed collider reference and ordinary object overhead. Maximum instance prefix is 480 records: 384 channel spans and 96 metal flecks. Each instance is 48 bytes. Tube storage is a 16-byte count header plus twelve 304-byte records, each holding eight finite spans and a conservative light bound.

| Resource | Bytes | Lifetime |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Zero-count arc storage binding | 320 | Renderer application |
| Arc ribbon/spark vertex instances | 23,040 | Lazy first strike; released on scene unload |
| Active finite-segment light storage | 3,664 | Lazy first strike; released on scene unload |
| Combined active arc buffers | 26,704 | Same scene lifetime |

The empty binding satisfies the storage layout without allocating a pool or full arc buffer in turret-only sessions. Shaders perform a zero-count loop check but no segment evaluation when inactive. The Arc render pipelines are created on first use and remain shared. There are no new full-screen passes or render targets. Active geometry adds one display draw; a living channel adds one source draw inside the existing bloom pass. If bloom itself has never been used, its existing shared resources are allocated normally.

The later approved generator plasma effect reuses these pipelines and finite-span lighting. When plasma is present, a separate 8,528-byte merged light buffer reserves all twelve Arc records plus sixteen plasma records; Arc capacity and arithmetic are preserved. Removing plasma restores the ordinary Arc binding and current light data. Plasma has its own instance pool and contributes a separate batch to the existing display/bloom passes. The table above remains the Arc-only allocation. [Plasma ownership, tuning and checks](engine.md#generator-plasma-connections-18-october).

Only active prefixes are uploaded. A single main strike and six flecks upload 1,824 instance bytes plus 320 light bytes; the bounded worst case is 23,040 + 3,664 bytes. Flying or fading flecks upload only their instance prefix after the channel expires. Light expiry uploads a zero-count 16-byte header once and invalidates opaque lighting once; idle frames have no Arc simulation loops, uploads or draws. Tube light changes refresh opaque shading, without invalidating geometry depth or shadows. A reset/unload drops attachment/collider references and destroys both active GPU buffers before replacement assets load. Renderer inspection exposes arc buffer sizes, strikes/sparks/grounded flecks/instances/lights and upload counts/bytes. These are resource/correctness observations, not GPU timing or frame-rate claims.
