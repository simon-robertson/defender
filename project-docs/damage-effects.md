## CPU ownership and collision

`engine/damage-effects.js` owns fixed reusable pools: **192 cubes, 128 smoke puffs and four shared blast/light/wave slots**. All per-frame data, sort storage and collision scratch are preallocated. Model palettes and emitter records are prepared only at scene load. Enemy `effectOrigin` selects an authored point on a rigid part: the dog uses body-local `[12,11.5,12]`. Reused part matrices place enemy damage smoke, cubes, flash, light and wave exactly at that animated centre. These enemy-origin effects have no spawn jitter or vertical offsets. Death locks its origin in world space. Full cube/smoke pools drop new particles. The blast pool now gives enemy deaths priority over the later missile contact flashes, as described above; an all-death pool retains the oldest-slot replacement rule. Diagnostics expose these counters.

The shared frame advances old effects, moves dogs, resolves turret shots and deaths, emits damage smoke, then renders. Death calls `explode(state)` before hiding the actor. The renderer packs effects after updating the current camera, sorting smoke by projected depth for both overview and perspective views. Cube transforms only repack/upload after motion, removal or reset. Resting cubes remain unchanged until expiry.

`engine/debris-collision.js` sweeps a conservative cube-enclosing radius through the existing static scenery/terrain BVH; there is no additional world collision structure. Bounded ballistic substeps and continuous sweeps prevent thin-wall tunnelling. Inelastic impacts damp linear and angular motion. A settling cube aligns flat onto a real supporting top face, eliminating the flight-bound hover gap. Stream water is not solid, so fragments land on its bed. Walls, rocks and foliage are solid according to authored occupied cells.

This is an efficient particle approximation, not a full rigid-body solver: no fragment-to-fragment or moving-actor collision, exact oriented cube contacts, or fluid interaction. Flight contacts are conservative; final resting orientation is snapped flat. Work is bounded to 120 substeps per moving cube after a long frame, integrating the elapsed interval; out-of-world fragments have the safety expiry above.

Scene unload resets all particles, emitter/palette references and the BVH reference before world disposal. Fixed application pools and lazily created GPU buffers remain reusable across zones; full renderer disposal destroys them. Dead enemies remain absent until R, refresh or leaving/returning to their zone; no automatic respawn or waves are added.

## Rendering and cost

`engine/damage-shaders.js` renders solid instanced cubes through the existing depth, directional-shadow and opaque passes. Cube faces use model lighting and zero reflectiveness. Their lit surfaces can appear in water SSR. Smoke uses procedural, softly edged alpha billboards; the brief blast uses additive blending. Both test opaque/water depth and reuse the existing display pass. Smoke responds to sky, ambient, shadows and local lights; the flash is emissive. These particles do not cast shadows or appear directly in SSR.

The existing merged point-light uniform includes eight muzzle slots, four explosion slots and twenty-one model slots. This tuning adds no light slots or buffer capacity. Explosion lights share the existing finite point-light falloff and are unshadowed, like muzzle lights. Lighting changes invalidate opaque colour without forcing camera-depth or shadow redraws. Moving cubes invalidate depth/shadow/opaque; their last expiry invalidates once to remove cached pixels.

Lazy GPU payload buffers total **21,696 bytes**: 15,360 for cube matrices/colours and 6,336 for smoke/blast instances. Five damage pipelines, with the later shockwave adding one lazy pipeline/target as described below. No new scene buffers. Active cubes add one instanced draw per required geometry pass; smoke and flash add at most two display draws. Empty effects add no payload uploads or draws. These are bounded operation/storage counts, not measured frame-rate claims.

## Refractive shockwave refinement

The user requested a longer blast and scene distortion from an expanding explosion shockwave. The blast now retains a warm tail through 0.55 seconds, with the hot core fading after the first 0.08 seconds. The wave shares the four existing blast slots and retains a fixed 128-byte CPU instance array. Its 3-unit shell expands to 32 units over 0.7 seconds; strength fades with age. Smoke, cubes, flash, light and wave originate at exactly the same transformed body centre. This is independent of the removed muzzle/weather pressure experiment; no particle forces are reintroduced.

`engine/shockwave-shaders.js` refracts a snapshot of the completed display, including water, SSR and all currently rendered particles. A narrow spherical rim offsets the sample; the interior and exterior remain unchanged. Destination and sampled-source depth protect opaque and water foreground, including silhouettes. Rays are reconstructed once per fragment and shared across up to four waves. There is no coloured ring or filled translucent bubble.

The renderer allocates one **144-byte uniform** and one **width × height × 4-byte** same-format colour target on first active use. Active frames render the existing display into that target, then add one fullscreen refraction draw. Payload writes are 16 + 32 bytes per active wave. Empty frames use the original direct-to-canvas route, with no wave upload or pass. The snapshot is retained for reuse until resize or scene clear, where it is destroyed; replacement is allocated only on the next wave. Pipeline/uniform persist across zones and are destroyed with the application.

Screen-space limits: transparent puffs/flashes/weather have no separate depth layer, so their foreground protection uses the underlying opaque/water depth. Refraction cannot recover off-screen or hidden imagery; out-of-screen samples fall back to the original pixel. These are deliberate bounded approximations, not fluid or pressure simulation.
