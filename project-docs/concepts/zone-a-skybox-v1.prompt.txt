Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: Zone A skybox art-direction concept for the existing Defender tower defence game; one polished 16:9 landscape image for design review, not a production cubemap.

Primary request: Show the game's sharp, rectangular playable landscape floating fairly high above an expansive vista of the SAME Moss and Porcelain biome. The distance should be unmistakable: the board is suspended hundreds of metres above the surrounding terrain, with a clear open gap below it. The skybox vista is darker and softly out of focus so attention stays on the gameplay area.

Foreground subject: A crisp, entire rectangular moss-green game board seen in an elevated three-quarter/isometric game view, with a thin exposed earthy stone slab edge, a winding pale limestone path, open deployment lawns, compact blocky oak and tall cypress trees, scattered porcelain limestone rocks and small ruined columns. Match a simple texture-free voxel/low-poly game: clean axis-aligned forms on the board, matte moss/olive foliage and warm pale stone. Keep the WHOLE gameplay board equally sharp and readable; no Drone camera depth of field or tilt-shift band across it. No game UI, labels, typography, units or characters.

Background: Far below and extending to the horizon, a broad low-poly woodland valley with faceted rolling green hills, dense simplified oak/cypress woods, a winding muted teal river and small pools, occasional pale limestone outcrops and understated old ruins. These should read as distant landforms and masses, not a second playable board. The biome continues well beyond the floating game area. Terrain and background props can use broad low-poly facets rather than detailed voxels.

Composition: The floating board is the clear hero, slightly above image centre, roughly half the image width. Show enough board top to read its winding path, a modest underside edge, and ample darker terrain beneath and around it to establish height and scale. Keep a coherent single perspective; no split panels, inset diagrams or floating duplicate islands.

Lighting and hierarchy: Clear daytime, gentle warm late-morning light on the board, its familiar moss and porcelain palette. Make the distant vista roughly a third darker, less saturated and lower contrast than the board. Calm muted sage-grey sky. Apply subtle optical softness ONLY to the distant skybox terrain and objects, progressively softer toward the horizon, while preserving recognisable low-poly silhouettes. The softness should imply distance without obscuring the biome. No dramatic fog banks, rain, night lighting, glowing fantasy effects, sci-fi structures, giant cliffs rising to board level, or physical supports connecting the board to the valley.
